# Preview animation

How every cover and animated figure for my case studies gets made: soft, looping product scenes
that end where they began.

**See it live:** the case-study covers and figures on [yuvrajsingh.work](https://yuvrajsingh.work).

Written for my portfolio repo, so its paths point there. It's here to show the process.

## The process

1. **Stills first.** Nothing moves until I've seen it still: each scene is rendered without its
   script and placed in my Figma as editable layers, never as a picture.
2. **Brief.** Every word and number comes from the real product, prototype or Figma. NDA rules are
   built in: a client's name becomes a bar, a phone number a fictional 555-01xx one.
3. **Story.** One sentence ("In this loop, someone does something and something happens"), three
   to five beats, and the seam picked before anything else. The poster is the first frame, so the
   loop starts on a frame that tells the story.
4. **Screen.** The product redesigned at 1:1 for the cover, simplified: 3 to 7 real strings where
   the story is, bars for the rest, nothing sliced by an edge.
5. **Choreograph.** Built as a scene with the Stageshow kit. Things arrive through the
   kit's moves, never by scrolling or crossfading screens, and the loop resets with a cause the
   viewer can see, never by playing backwards. 8 to 10 seconds for a cover.
6. **Rehearse.** Stageshow's film renders a contact sheet of the loop; every line it flags gets
   fixed at its cause.
7. **Record.** Frame-exact on Stageshow's virtual clock at 60 fps: exactly one loop to MP4 and WebM
   with a poster, checked for length, seam, flashes, colour, file weight and reduced motion.

A screen recording can stand in for a scene when the real feel is the point, like a live voice
call, with the same checks.

  rules, Figma review, recording and export.
